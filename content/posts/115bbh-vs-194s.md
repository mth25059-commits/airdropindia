People mix these up constantly. They're actually two completely different concepts that happen to apply to the same trade.

## Section 115BBH — the 30% tax

- Introduced by the **Finance Act, 2022**, effective **1 April 2022** (FY 2022-23).
- Applies to "income from the transfer of any Virtual Digital Asset".
- **Rate:** 30% flat.
- Plus the standard Health & Education Cess of 4% on the tax.
- Plus surcharge if you're high-income.
- **No deductions** other than cost of acquisition. No fees, no expenses.
- **No set-off:** losses on one VDA can't reduce gains on another.
- **No carry-forward:** if you have a net loss, it disappears.

This is the tax you actually pay.

## Section 194S — the 1% TDS

- Also introduced by the Finance Act, 2022, effective **1 July 2022**.
- Requires the **payer** (typically the exchange or counterparty) to withhold 1% of the consideration paid for a VDA transfer.
- **Threshold:** ₹10,000 of aggregate consideration in a financial year, ₹50,000 for "specified persons".
- This is *not* an additional tax — it's a deduction at source, credited back to you in your ITR.

This is **collection**, not tax. It's the IT department's way of making sure they get paid even if you forget to.

## How they work together

You sell ETH worth ₹1,00,000 to a buyer. You bought it earlier for ₹70,000.

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| Profit | ₹30,000 |
| 115BBH base tax (30%) | ₹9,000 |
| Cess (4%) | ₹360 |
| **Total tax payable to govt** | **₹9,360** |
| 194S TDS (1%) deducted by buyer | ₹1,000 |
| You receive after TDS | ₹99,000 |
| When filing ITR, you claim back the ₹1,000 as TDS credit | |
| Net tax you actually owe | ₹9,360 − ₹1,000 = **₹8,360** |

So 194S doesn't *add* tax. It just collects part of it in advance.

## Edge cases

**Foreign exchanges (Binance, Bybit):** They don't deduct Indian TDS. You're supposed to compute and pay it yourself as the deductor. Practically, most users skip this — at your own risk.

**P2P trades:** Same story — the buyer is the deductor. If you sell crypto on Binance P2P, technically the buyer should deduct 1% TDS and remit it. In practice this never happens.

**Self-transfers and wallet movements:** Not a "transfer". No tax, no TDS.

**Gifts of crypto:** If from a non-relative or above ₹50,000 in aggregate, taxed as "income from other sources" at slab rate. Then 115BBH kicks in when you sell.

## The bottom line

- **115BBH = your real tax bill** (30% + cess).
- **194S = the IT department's prepayment hook** (1% withheld, you claim it back).
