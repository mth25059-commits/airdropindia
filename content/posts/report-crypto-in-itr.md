Filing crypto in your ITR isn't complicated. It's just unusually strict — every transfer goes in its own row, with five columns. Here's how to do it without panic.

## Which ITR form?

- **ITR-2** — if you have only capital gains and crypto income, no business.
- **ITR-3** — if you also run a business (freelance, consulting, etc.).

Most retail users file ITR-2.

## Schedule VDA — the crypto schedule

The Income-tax Department added **Schedule VDA** specifically for crypto. You report each transfer separately:

| Field | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Date of acquisition** | When you bought (or received) the token |
| **Date of transfer** | When you sold, swapped, or gifted it |
| **Cost of acquisition** | What you paid in INR, ignoring fees |
| **Consideration received** | Sale value in INR |
| **Income from transfer** | Sale − Cost (profit; can be negative but the tax on it is zero) |

You don't have to lump everything together — each transaction is a row.

## How TDS shows up

The 1% TDS deducted by Indian exchanges shows up in **Form 26AS** under the section for TDS. When filing:

1. Open Form 26AS at the income-tax e-filing portal.
2. Check the "TDS on payment of VDA u/s 194S" entries.
3. The same amount auto-fills in **Schedule TDS** of your ITR.
4. The tax payable on Schedule VDA (30% + cess) minus the TDS credit = what you actually owe.

If TDS > tax, you get a refund.

## What you need ready

Before opening the ITR form, pull these out of every exchange (CoinDCX, WazirX, Binance, etc.):

1. **Transaction history** for the whole FY — date, asset, side (buy/sell/swap), INR value.
2. **TDS statements** (most exchanges generate them as PDF).
3. **Deposit/withdrawal logs** if relevant.
4. **Wallet addresses you used** — useful if the IT department asks for proof.

## Foreign exchange problem

If you trade on Binance, Bybit, OKX etc.:

- **They don't deduct Indian TDS.** Technically you're the "buyer" obligation falls on the Indian person paying you, but for crypto transfers between two non-residents, the rules are murky.
- Conservative approach: compute 1% on every sell yourself, deposit it with the IT department via challan, and claim it back in your ITR. Few people do this in practice.
- The IT department has been issuing notices to people with high-volume foreign exchange activity — keep records.

## Common mistakes

1. **Netting losses against profits** — illegal under 115BBH. Each profit is taxed separately.
2. **Treating swaps as non-events** — every swap is a transfer. Both the sell of token A and the buy of token B need to be valued in INR.
3. **Forgetting TDS credit** — you paid it, claim it back.
4. **Filing under "income from other sources"** at slab rate to save tax — also illegal. The Act mandates Schedule VDA.

## Final sanity check

Before submitting:

- All profits sum up correctly in Schedule VDA
- Tax = 31.2% × sum of profits (+ surcharge if applicable)
- TDS credit matches Form 26AS
- No losses are netted against profits
- All foreign exchange trades are also included
