If you bought, sold, swapped, or even gifted crypto in India this financial year, the income-tax law has a clear opinion about it. Here is the whole story, in plain English.

## The two laws you need to know

**Section 115BBH** (introduced by the Finance Act 2022, effective 1 April 2022)

- A flat **30% tax** on profit from the *transfer* of any Virtual Digital Asset (VDA) — that's the legal name for crypto, NFTs, and most tokens.
- Add **4% Health & Education Cess** on the 30%. Your real rate is **31.2%**.
- **No deductions** except the cost of acquisition. You can't subtract trading fees, gas costs, internet bills, your CA's fee — nothing.
- **Losses cannot offset other income.** If you lost ₹50,000 on a memecoin and made ₹50,000 on Bitcoin, you still pay 31.2% tax on the Bitcoin profit. The loss is dead capital.
- **Losses cannot be carried forward.** Same logic — they just vanish for tax purposes.

**Section 194S** (also 2022)

- A flat **1% TDS** withheld at source when you sell crypto in India.
- Threshold: ₹10,000 of aggregate sells in the financial year for most people.
- For "specified persons" (small individuals/HUFs with no business income and total turnover under prescribed limits), the threshold is **₹50,000**.
- Indian exchanges (CoinDCX, WazirX, etc.) deduct this automatically. You get it back as a TDS credit when filing ITR.

## What counts as "transfer"?

Lots of things, even if no INR moves:

- Selling crypto for INR
- Swapping one crypto for another (yes, even ETH → USDT)
- Spending crypto to buy goods
- Gifting crypto in some cases

Receiving crypto as a gift, salary, airdrop, or mining reward is **income** at fair value on the day you received it, taxable at slab rates *separately* from the 30% rule. Then later when you sell, 115BBH kicks in.

## A quick example

You buy 0.01 BTC at ₹40,00,000 per BTC, total cost ₹40,000.

A few months later, you sell at ₹50,00,000 per BTC, total sale ₹50,000.

- Profit: ₹10,000
- Tax (30% + 4% cess) = ₹3,120
- TDS (1% of ₹50,000) = ₹500
- Net cash in hand: ₹50,000 − ₹3,120 − ₹500 = ₹46,380

## What about airdrops?

Two taxable events:

1. **On receipt** — if the fair market value at the moment of receiving the token is determinable, it's "income from other sources" at your slab rate. Most CAs in India treat zero-value testnet drops as no-event, but cash-equivalent drops as taxable on day 1.
2. **On sale** — the cost of acquisition is the FMV used in step 1. Profit thereafter is 30% under 115BBH. Plus 1% TDS if the sale crosses the threshold.

## ITR reporting

In your ITR you fill **Schedule VDA** (Virtual Digital Assets). Each transaction goes in with date of acquisition, date of transfer, cost, sale value, and profit. Your TDS deducted by exchanges shows up in **Form 26AS** — you claim it back as a TDS credit in the return.

## Three rules to live by

1. **Set aside 31.2% on every win.** Don't spend the gross — treat 31.2% as already-spent the moment you book a profit.
2. **Keep a clean trade log.** Date, asset, quantity, INR value at buy, INR value at sell. Export it monthly. CAs love this.
3. **Don't try to net losses.** If you trade two assets and one loses, you can't reduce the tax on the winner. Plan around it.

## TL;DR

| Thing | Rate / Rule |
|---|---|
| Profit on transfer of VDA | 30% flat |
| Cess on the tax | 4% (effective 31.2%) |
| Loss set-off / carry-forward | Not allowed |
| TDS on sells > ₹10k in FY | 1% (Section 194S) |
| ITR schedule | Schedule VDA |
